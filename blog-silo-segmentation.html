What "Silo" Gets Right About Your Security Stack, and Your Marketing Org

Segmentation without shared visibility isn't zero trust. It's a bunker with better branding.

Brittney Lawrence

If you've watched Apple TV's Silo, you already know the premise, even if you've never worked in cybersecurity or B2B marketing. Thousands of people live in a massive underground bunker, split into rigid levels. Information about the outside world is tightly controlled. Asking too many questions about what's happening a few floors away, or what happened generations ago, is treated as dangerous. Even the small things are engineered against you: the elevators barely run, not because anyone benefits from that, but because friction between levels keeps people from moving up, or finding out too much about what's above them.

Now the surprising part: I've sat in enough security reviews and GTM planning meetings to tell you that's not science fiction. That's Tuesday.

Segmentation is good. Blindness is not.

Let's start with the literal security lesson, because it's a real one. Network segmentation exists for a good reason: contain a breach, limit the blast radius, keep one compromised system from taking down everything connected to it. I've written about this exact principle for small businesses. Segment your systems, and a single ransomware infection doesn't have to become a company-wide catastrophe.

But Silo's residents aren't just segmented from each other physically. They're segmented from information. Nobody on one level knows what's really happening on another, and like those elevators, that isn't an accident of design, it's the design. Nobody has the full picture, on purpose, and that's exactly what lets the actual threat operate undetected for generations. Here's the difference between segmentation that protects you and segmentation that just blinds you:

Segmentation done rightSegmentation done like Silo
SystemsIsolated to contain breachesIsolated to contain breaches
InformationShared across teams monitoring the whole environmentLocked inside each team's own level
Result when something's wrongCaught fast, cross-team responseNobody has enough context to notice until it's a crisis

If your SOC, your IT team, and your leadership can't see the same picture, you haven't built zero trust. You've built a bunker with better branding.

The part that's actually about your marketing org

Here's where it gets uncomfortable for a lot of B2B teams, especially at security and AI companies where the product is genuinely hard to explain: your GTM org is often run exactly like Silo. Product knows what's actually being built. Sales knows what customers are actually asking for and objecting to. Marketing knows how the category is actually being talked about externally. Security or compliance knows what claims are actually defensible. Most companies run these as separate levels, each with its own version of the truth, each a little suspicious of what the other levels are doing.

Sound familiar? It's the same failure mode, just wearing a different uniform. Not a technology problem, an information-silo problem, and it produces the same result: nobody notices the actual threat, in this case a positioning gap, a stalled pipeline, a competitor quietly redefining the category, until it's already a crisis. It usually looks something like this: product ships a feature, sales doesn't know how to position it yet, marketing doesn't even know it shipped, the content goes six months stale, and a competitor names the category first while everyone was still waiting on the next floor to say something.

Why one person owning the full picture is the actual fix

This is exactly why the fractional model works better for technical, category-defining companies than hiring three separate specialists who each own one level. A content person who's never touched the product roadmap can't catch the gap. A strategist without technical depth can't tell when a claim isn't defensible. Someone who only does sales enablement never sees the search data showing the narrative isn't landing.

You don't fix a Silo problem by adding another wall. You fix it by putting someone in the loop who's actually allowed to see every level, whose job is connecting them into one story instead of defending their own floor. That's not a metaphor I'm reaching for to make a point, it's the actual reason "fractional head of product marketing" beats another specialist hire for companies that can't afford to have their positioning, their pipeline, and their technical accuracy each managed by people who never talk to each other.

Brittney Lawrence is a fractional product marketing leader for security and AI companies, and yes, she has opinions about prestige sci-fi.
